3 · Read & play

The reader's method

AI writes code faster than any human. That moved the scarce skill: the bottleneck now is people who can read code and judge it. This lesson is deliberate practice at exactly that.

The method: read a line, predict out loud what it does, then run and check. A wrong prediction is the best moment of your session — it's the exact spot where your model of the code differs from reality, found cheaply.

Two power tools. The deletion test: unsure what a line does? Delete it, refresh, observe what broke, undo. The browser forgives everything. And the explain-it-back prompt: paste a line to your AI — "explain this like I've never seen it, then quiz me with one question." The quiz keeps you honest.

Style-guide note for the whole course: every file you keep should read like you wrote it — your indenting, your comments, no mystery lines. A mystery line kept is a debt future-you pays.

4 · Do it

The mystery page

1. Ask your AI: "generate a 30–40 line HTML page about the history of Chicago's flag, using at least two tags a beginner wouldn't know."

2. Before opening it in the browser: annotate every line with a prediction comment.

3. Open it. Score your predictions honestly.

4. Deletion-test the two unfamiliar tags; write down what actually broke.
