3 · Read & play

The reading school and the manners school

Phase one: pretraining. The model read a gigantic corpus of text and learned, by next-word practice, the patterns inside it — grammar, facts, styles, code. Two consequences matter to you forever after:

First, the knowledge cutoff. The reading ended on a date. The model's built-in knowledge is frozen there — it knows history up to the cutoff and nothing after. Tools that answer about yesterday are bolting live web search onto the frozen model; know which one you're talking to.

Test your feel for where the library ends:

Interactive · Figure 1

The frozen library

Six questions. Call each one before you flip it: can the model know this from its reading alone — no web search bolted on?

Second, it learned what people wrote, not what is true. Where the internet is rich and accurate, the model tends to be too. Where the internet is thin, wrong, or lopsided, the model inherited that as well — which becomes a whole lesson later in this course.

Phase two: alignment (the manners school). After reading, humans spent months shaping behavior: rating answers, rewarding helpfulness and honesty, training refusals for harmful requests. When a model says "I can't help with that," you're hearing this phase. It's also why models say "I don't know" at all — raw prediction would rather guess; it was taught to admit uncertainty, and the teaching is imperfect.

Raw prediction continues whatever it is given — the internet contains every one of those voices. Months of human ratings taught the second column. When a model declines politely or admits it does not know, you are hearing the school.

Frozen library + etiquette lessons. Keep both in view and a lot of AI behavior stops being mysterious.

4 · Try it

Find the edge of the library

1. Ask the model directly: "What is your knowledge cutoff date?"

2. Probe both sides of the edge. Ask about something well before the cutoff (it should be strong), then about something after it. Watch how it handles not knowing — does it admit it, or guess?

3. Meet the manners school. Ask for something a well-behaved assistant should refuse (say, someone else's home address). Read the refusal closely — that text is trained behavior, not reading.

5 · Quick check

Check yourself

1. Why can't the model reliably discuss last week's news from its own knowledge?

2. An AI refuses a harmful request. Where does that behavior come from?

3. From lesson 2 — a tool answers questions about yesterday accurately. Given the cutoff, what's happening?

6 · Build

Interview the machine about itself

Interview the AI about its own limits — at least eight questions. Push past the first answer: what kinds of things can't you know? what happens when you're not sure? what were you trained on?

Then write the five most interesting limits it admitted, in your own words, and mark the one you plan to verify yourself — because after lesson 6, you won't be taking its word for things.

Turn-in: your five limits + the one you'll test.
