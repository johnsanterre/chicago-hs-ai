3 · Read & play

Assembling the machine

Everything is built; today it clicks together. The runtime loop is four moves long:

1. Embed the incoming question (lesson 4).
2. Rank chunks by nearness; keep the top few — the top-k, usually three to five (lesson 4).
3. Build the prompt: the question plus the retrieved chunks, each with its receipt (lesson 2).
4. Send it to the model and return the answer.

Move 4 is where this lesson's real content lives, because the prompt is not "here's some stuff, answer the question." It is a contract, and each clause exists because of a specific failure it prevents:

"Answer using ONLY the provided sections." Without it, the model blends your chunks with its training-data guesses — other schools' retake rules leak into Jefferson's answer, and you can't see the seam.
"Cite the section after each fact." The receipts traveled from chunking through retrieval precisely so the answer could carry them. A cited answer is checkable in seconds; an uncited one is a rumor.
"If the sections don't contain the answer, say exactly that." This clause does the most work. Without it, a model handed the wrong chunks does what models do: writes something plausible anyway. With it, retrieval failure surfaces as an honest "not in the provided documents" instead of a confident invention — a failure you can see and fix beats one you swallow without noticing.

That is the whole architecture this course promised: your pile stays in your storage, retrieval picks the relevant atoms, and the model — under contract — turns them into a cited answer. The figure walks one question through every station.

6 · Build

Ask your pile ten questions

Point the assembled ask() at your own pile and run your ten-question set. For each answer, record: right or wrong, cited or not, and — for any 'not in the documents' response — whether that refusal was correct (the answer truly isn't there) or a retrieval miss (it is there and wasn't found). Those two kinds of refusal look identical to a casual reader; you now have the receipts to tell them apart.
