3 · Read & play

The list, the loop, the indent

A list holds values in order: crew = ["Maya", "DeShawn", "Alex"]. Positions count from zero — crew[0] is Maya. Every programmer has tripped on that once; now you won't.

A for loop does something once per item: for person in crew: followed by an indented line. The indent is the loop body — it's what repeats. Python is strict about it, and the strictness is a favor: you can see the shape of the program.

range(100) gives a loop 100 turns — the machinery behind lesson 1's print-your-name-100-times build. And the collector pattern — total = total + n inside a loop — is how programs count, sum, and accumulate anything.

The notebook ends by asking the AI for a faster way to sum a million numbers, then checking both answers agree. Verifying the intern's work — that habit is the whole course.
