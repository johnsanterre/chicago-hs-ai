Verification: try to kill every claim

Sources disagree, pages go stale, and models invent. Before a claim reaches the report, a verifier reads the sources trying to refute it — and the claim survives on evidence, not on confidence.

3 · Read & play

Guilty until sourced

Everything upstream of this stage can lie politely. A page can be stale — the garden's own history page still says 48 plots, because nobody updated it. A page can be wrong — the mini-web's blog post says the garden has 600 plots, a typo that survived because blogs have no editor. And the model that extracted your claims can invent — a plausible date, a rounded number the source never said. If you took Document Pipelines, this is the validation gate again, rebuilt for research: the place where the lying stops.

The adversarial move. Asking the model "is this claim right?" invites agreement — models like to say yes. So the verifier is prompted the opposite way: here is a claim and here are the sources; find the evidence that contradicts it. Trying to kill the claim and failing is what support means. This one prompt-design choice — refute, don't confirm — is most of what makes verification real.

The vote. One verification pass can itself be wrong, so each claim gets three independent passes, and the majority decides. Sources get weighed while they're at it: two independent pages agreeing beat one blog post, a 2026 census beats a 2023 sign, and a source that copied another source counts once, not twice — the dedup receipts from lesson 4 are what make that visible.

What survives. Each claim leaves this stage labeled: confirmed (survived all three passes), plausible (survived with a caveat worth keeping), or refuted (killed, with the evidence that killed it kept on file). Refuted claims are never deleted — they are quarantined with their reason, because "what the agent decided not to believe, and why" is part of an honest report.

Check yourself

6 · Build

Break the verifier

The notebook's mini-web has one contradiction seeded on purpose. Add a second: edit one page in the corpus to plant a wrong fact that the current claim table depends on, rerun the pipeline from gather through verify, and report what happened. Did the vote catch your plant? If it survived, explain what property of your edit slipped past the checks — that explanation is the real lesson.

Turn-in: your planted edit, the rerun verdicts, and three sentences on why it was caught or missed.
