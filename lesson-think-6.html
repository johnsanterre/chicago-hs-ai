Break down big things

Big assignments don't stall because they're hard. They stall because they don't fit in a day. Splitting is the skill — and the AI is a strong splitting partner when you push back.

3 · Read & play

Split until it fits

You know the feeling: "research project due in four weeks" sits in your head as one giant object, too big to start, so you don't. The problem isn't effort. The problem is that "do the project" is not a task. It's a category of tasks pretending to be one.

The fix is decomposition: split the thing, then split the pieces, until every leaf passes two tests — it fits in one sitting, and it has a visible finish ("three sources found and saved" — you know when it's done; "work on research" — you never know when it's done).

The AI is a strong splitting partner. Give it the whole assignment and your real constraints: "Break this into tasks. I have about 45 minutes on weekdays. For each task, tell me what finished looks like." Then do the three things it can't:

Push back on lazy splits. "Step 2 'do the research' is still three tasks. Split it."
Add what it can't know. Your practice schedule, your slow subjects, the week your cousins visit.
Own the calendar. The AI proposes an order; you put dates on it, and you put the scary task early — not because the AI said so, but because week four is a bad time to discover the hard part.

When a step stalls mid-project, that's not failure — it's information: the step was secretly three steps. Split it again and keep going.

4 · Try it

Split something real

Take the biggest thing actually looming in your life — a project, an application, an event you're organizing. Hand it to the AI with your real constraints and get a split.

Then earn it: find and fix at least two lazy splits, add at least one step the AI couldn't know about, and put real dates on the result.

5 · Quick check
