3 · Read & play

A repair manual, not a mystery

Your question set has misses. Good — misses with receipts are diagnosable, and there are only about four mechanisms behind nearly all of them.

The answer straddled a cut. The rule in one chunk, its condition in the next; neither alone scores well. Fix at the chunking stage: bigger chunks along real seams, more overlap (lesson 2's knife, re-swung).

Top-k too small. The right chunk ranked fourth; you kept three. Fix: raise k — and know the cost curve. More chunks means more context spent and more noise for the model to ignore; k is a dial, not a virtue.

The question's phrasing lands far from the answer's. Embeddings shrink the synonym gap; they don't erase it. A question full of slang or missing key terms can still land in the wrong region. Fix: query rewriting — a cheap model call that turns "so like when can we bail early" into "what time is early dismissal" before embedding. The model fixes the question, retrieval stays honest.

The answer isn't in the pile. No retrieval fixes absence. Fix: either add the missing document, or let the refusal stand — that edge is real, and lesson 6 taught why honoring it beats papering over it.

The discipline tying it together: never tune blind. One stage changed at a time, the full question set rerun after, hits counted before and after. Tuning by vibes — "it feels better now" — is how systems drift worse while everyone nods. If you took Build with LLMs, this is the eval lesson again, now with more stages to blame; the measuring habit transfers whole.

6 · Build

Fix your two worst misses

From your audit: take your two worst misses, diagnose each against the four mechanisms, apply the matching fix, and rerun your full ten-question set — not just the fixed questions, because fixes can break hits (a re-chunk that saves one question can orphan another's answer). Report both before/after tables.

Turn-in: the two diagnoses, the fixes, and both tables — with one sentence on anything a fix broke.
