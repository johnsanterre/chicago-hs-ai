3 · Read & play

Count what follows what

Here is the plan for this whole course: you are going to build a language model. A small one — it fits in a free Colab notebook and trains in seconds — but a real one, built on the same idea as the giant ones you use every day: look at what came before, place bets on what comes next.

Version one needs no math beyond counting. Take some text. Walk through it one letter at a time and keep a tally: after the letter t, how often does h come next? After q, how often u? Do that for every pair of letters and you get a table. That table can answer a question no program you've written could answer before: "given this letter, what probably comes next?"

That's a language model. Not a metaphor for one — an actual one, the kind called a bigram model ("bigram" = two things in a row). It knows nothing about meaning, grammar, or the world. It knows what follows what, because it counted. Everything else in this course is upgrades to this one move.
