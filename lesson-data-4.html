3 · Read

groupby, and the caveat that keeps you honest

df.groupby('neighborhood')['score'].mean() reads as a sentence: by neighborhood, the average of score. Swap any slot — groupby('grade')['hours'].max() — and you've asked a different question. This one pattern answers most "compare the groups" questions that exist.

Combine with lesson 2's moves and you can interrogate anything: filter to heavy studiers, then average their scores; count students per neighborhood, then sort. The analysis sentence after each result remains mandatory.

Now the honesty rule that separates analysts from headline writers. When two things move together — study hours up, scores up — the table alone cannot tell you why. Maybe studying causes scores. Maybe a third thing (tutors, sleep, a quiet home) drives both. The professional phrasing is "associated with"; the word "causes" is reserved for experiments where you changed one thing on purpose. Every claim in your build carries a caveat sentence. That's not weakness — it's the credibility.
