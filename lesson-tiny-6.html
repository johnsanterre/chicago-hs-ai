3 · Read & play

Merge, repeat

Letters were the honest place to start, but they make everything slow: seventy rolls of the dice to write one short line. Words seem better — until you meet a word the vocabulary lacks, and the model is simply stuck. Real models split the difference with tokens: chunks bigger than letters, smaller than words, and — this is the good part — the chunks are not designed by anyone. They're discovered, by counting.

The algorithm is called byte-pair encoding, and you already have the skills for it:

Start with text split into single characters.
Count every adjacent pair. Find the most frequent one — say t+h.
Merge it everywhere: "th" is now one token in the vocabulary.
Repeat. Next round, maybe "th"+"e" merges into "the". Keep going and the vocabulary fills with the building blocks of the actual text: common words become single tokens, rare words stay in pieces.

Run it long enough on enough text and you get vocabularies like the real ones — tens of thousands of tokens. And now course 1's oddities crack open: why models can miscount the letters in a word (they see token-chunks, not letters), why weird spellings confuse them (rare words shatter into many pieces), and why "strawberry" is a different object to the model than it is to you.
