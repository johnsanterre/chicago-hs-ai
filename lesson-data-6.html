3 · Read

The two workhorse lies

Most chart deception uses true numbers. The lie lives in the presentation, and two techniques do most of the work:

The chopped axis. Start bars at 70 instead of 0 and a three-point drift becomes a skyscraper. Pair it with an excited title and the reader's takeaway is false while every number is true.

The cherry-picked window. Any wiggly line contains a stretch that goes up. Show only that stretch and "attendance is rising" — inside a year-long decline. The choice of window is itself a claim, and it's the one nobody prints.

Your defense is the spotter's checklist: Where does the axis start? What time window was chosen, and what would the wider view show? How big is the change in absolute terms? Who benefits from this framing? Four questions, reflexive, forever — and after building these lies with your own hands today, the reflex will be real. Analysts who know how charts lie are also the ones trusted not to.
