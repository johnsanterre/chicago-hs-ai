Research and verify

The AI can map a topic in a minute. It can also invent a source without blinking. Using it for research means holding both facts at once.

3 · Read & play

Map, then check

For getting oriented in a new topic, the AI is the best tool ever built. Ask "what are the main sides of the school-start-time debate, the key terms, and the questions people argue about?" and in thirty seconds you have a map that would have taken an afternoon of searching. Use it for that constantly.

Then the rule changes. A map shows you where to look. It is not a place to stand. The AI is a starting point, never a source — because it produces wrong facts in exactly the same confident tone as right ones, and because it can fabricate a citation out of thin air: real-sounding author, plausible title, proper journal formatting, page numbers. The formatting proves nothing. Formatting is the one thing it never gets wrong.

So the workflow is two passes. Pass one — map: get the overview, the vocabulary, the claims. Pass two — verify: pick out every claim that matters to your work and check each one against a source you can actually open and point at.

Which claims matter? The rule of thumb: anything with a number, a name, a date, or a quote gets checked before you repeat it. Background framing ("the debate has two main camps") is low-risk. "The study followed 9,000 students and found a 4.5% improvement" is exactly the kind of sentence that is sometimes real, sometimes almost-real, and sometimes pure invention.

4 · Try it

Catch it lying about something you know

Round 1: ask for a detailed answer about something you know deeply — your neighborhood, your team's season, a game you've mastered. Hunt for the errors. Finding them in familiar territory teaches you what they look like in unfamiliar territory.

Round 2: ask about a topic from your actual schoolwork. Apply the rule: every number, name, date, and quote gets a real source before it goes in your notes. Track how many survive.

6 · Build

A verified brief

Turn in the round-2 brief: the AI's answer with every claim marked safe or check-it, and a real, openable source listed for at least three checked claims — including any claim that turned out to be wrong.

Turn-in: the marked-up brief. The wrong claims are the most valuable part; bring one to class.
