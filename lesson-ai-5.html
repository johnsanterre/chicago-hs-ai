2 · Listen

Listen: two minutes before you go on

Treat the AI like a talented intern: push the draft, show it examples, make it critique itself, and always ask for options. (Generated narration; produced audio comes later.)

3 · Read

The editor's moves

Beginners send one message and take what comes back. That's like a coach watching one play and going home. The conversation is the tool — every message you add is information the model uses to sharpen the next draft.

Iterate. React to the draft the way you'd coach an intern: shorter, warmer, less formal, cut the second paragraph, stronger ending. Each instruction costs seconds and compounds.

Show examples. Descriptions of style are weak; samples are strong. Paste two or three examples of the thing you want — "match the voice of these" — and watch the output snap toward them. Showing beats telling.

Flip the critic. Ask it to attack its own work: "What are the three weakest points in what you just wrote?" It's usually a sharper critic than writer — then make it fix exactly what it found.

Ask for options. One safe, one bold, one weird. Options move you from accepting or rejecting to choosing — and choosing is where your taste shows up. Your taste is the part the machine doesn't have.

4 · Try it

Push a draft through five rounds

Choose something you actually want to exist — a club announcement, a bio for a profile, a birthday message that doesn't sound canned.

Round 1: your best lesson-4 prompt. Rounds 2–3: iterate with specific coaching. Round 4: flip the critic and make it fix what it found. Round 5: ask for two final options and choose.

Keep the whole transcript — it's your evidence.

5 · Quick check

Check yourself

1. What's the most effective way to get output in a specific style?

2. What does 'flip the critic' mean?

3. From lesson 4 — the draft came back ten paragraphs long when you wanted three sentences. Which lever did you forget?

6 · Build

The before-and-after

Turn in the full five-round transcript plus a two-sentence caption: which move improved the draft most, and what you chose between at the end.

Grade yourself honestly: if round 5 isn't clearly better than round 1, run it again with harder coaching.
