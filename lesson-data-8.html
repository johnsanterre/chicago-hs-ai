3 · Read

The rules of the investigation

Question first. Written down before you touch data. It's allowed to evolve — but starting without one turns analysis into wandering.

Log the cleaning. Every change, listed (lesson 3). The log is what lets anyone — teacher, teammate, future nonprofit client — trust what came out.

Analyze toward the question. Filters, groupbys, counts — each result followed by its plain-English sentence.

Chart honestly. Claim titles, labeled axes, zero-based bars, no cherry-picked windows — you know the lies now; that knowledge is a responsibility.

The finding is the deliverable. One paragraph: what you found, how confident you are, and the caveat — what your data can't tell you and what would change your mind. Code backs the paragraph, not the other way around. This exact shape — question, evidence, honest finding — is what you'll hand a real organization one day. Practice it like it counts, because it does.
