3 · Read

Four cases for the off switch

1. When practice is the point. Some work exists to produce a thing — use every tool you have. Other work exists to produce a change in you: the essay that teaches you to write, the problem set that builds the muscle. Having AI do those is sending a robot to lift your weights — the bar moves, you don't. Before reaching for the tool, ask: what is this assignment actually for?

2. When it must be right and you can't verify. Health, law, money, safety — anywhere a hallucination lands on a real person. The rule from lesson 6 hardens here: verify with a real source, or don't use it. There is no third option, and "it sounded sure" is not a source.

3. When the information isn't yours to share. A friend's secret, your family's finances, and — soon, in the practicum courses — a nonprofit's donor data. Chat windows are not vaults. The professional habit: before pasting, ask whose information is this, and did they say yes?

4. When the moment is human. Apologies, condolences, the message to a friend having their worst week. These count precisely because a person did them. Eleven honest words from you beat three hundred polished ones from a machine, every time.

That's the course. You know what the machine is (a predictor), how it reads (tokens on a desk), where its knowledge comes from and stops (the frozen library), how to steer it (four levers, editor's moves), how it fails (fluently), whose patterns it mirrors (ours), and now — when to put it down. The next four courses on this site put the tool in your hands to build. You're ready.
