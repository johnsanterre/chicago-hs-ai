3 · Read & play

Sharp and brittle

The structure stage begins with rules: text patterns that match the shape of what you want. Three workhorses cover most organizational documents:

Amounts: a dollar sign, digits, maybe commas and cents — \$[\d,]+(\.\d\d)? grabs $312.75 and $1,847.50 from any report you'll meet.
Dates: after lesson 3 standardized them, one pattern matches every date in the pile — this is why cleaning came first.
Emails: something, an @, something, a dot, something. Contact info practically extracts itself.

Where formats are fixed, rules are unbeatable: fast, free, perfectly consistent, and they never invent anything — a rule can only find what's literally there. A form that always says "Families served:" followed by a number will yield to one rule, forever.

Where humans got creative, rules crumble. "Due: next Tuesday." "We raised around two grand." "Contact Rosa — she knows." No pattern matches meaning; rules match shapes, and these have the wrong shape. You can add rules for a while — then the rulebook itself becomes the mess. Knowing where that boundary sits is the skill, and the next lesson is what lives on the other side of it.
