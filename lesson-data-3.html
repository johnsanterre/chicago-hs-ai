3 · Read

Diagnose, decide, disclose

Real-world data arrives dirty, always: missing values, inconsistent spelling ('Pilsen' / 'pilsen' / 'PILSEN' counting as three places), duplicate rows, impossible values (-1 hours online). Cleaning is most of real data work — and it's judgment, not just commands.

Diagnose first: df.isna().sum() counts missing per column; df['col'].unique() exposes spelling chaos; df.duplicated().sum() finds copies. Professionals look before they touch.

Fix one problem at a time, checking after each: drop_duplicates(), str.title().str.strip() to unify text, and impossible values converted to missing — a lie is worse than a blank.

Decide and disclose. Missing values force a choice — drop the rows, fill with an average, or leave them — and no choice is automatically right; each bends the answers differently. So you keep a cleaning log: every change, listed, in order. Cleaned data with a log is science. Without one, it's just a table somebody quietly tampered with.
