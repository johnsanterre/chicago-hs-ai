3 · Read

The four requirements

The method is the same one every capstone in these courses has drilled: plan in comments before code, build in pieces, sign for every line the AI helped with, and it runs twice, clean. What's new is what shipping an AI product demands beyond working code:

The system prompt, explained. It's the product's design and its limits — present it like one, with a sentence on each rule it enforces.

One eval check. A single automated property test (lesson 6) that proves the helper holds one promise. One check honestly run beats ten claimed virtues.

The cost line. Total tokens spent, printed by your code. You built on a shared key; accounting for what you used is professional habit one.

The honesty note. A paragraph on what it does badly — where it hallucinates, what it can't know, when a human should take over. Every real AI product has this paragraph; the trustworthy ones say it out loud. Yours will, from the first thing you ever ship.
