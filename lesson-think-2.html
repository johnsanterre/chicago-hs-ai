Asking well — and asking again

3 · Read & play

The second message

A good first ask says four things: your situation, exactly what you want, what shape the answer should take, and what the AI should not do. That takes fifteen seconds and doubles the quality of what comes back.

But the real skill starts after the answer arrives, because the first answer is almost never the best one available. You have three moves:

Steer. Say what's wrong and what direction to go: "closer to the second option, but shorter, and drop the jokes." Vague reactions ("make it better") steer nothing. Specific reactions steer everything.

Show an example. Paste something in the style you want — a paragraph you like, a format from class — and say "match this." One example beats three sentences of description.

Start clean. When a chat has gone wrong — the AI has latched onto a bad assumption and keeps building on it — stop steering. Open a fresh chat and write a better first ask using everything you learned from the wreck. Knowing when to restart is what separates people who use AI well from people who argue with it.

One more move, free: end a hard ask with "ask me two questions before you answer if anything is unclear." The model is good at knowing what it's missing — when you invite it to ask.

4 · Try it

Three second-messages

Ask the AI for something real — a study plan, an explanation, a draft. Whatever comes back, improve it three separate ways:

Round A: steer it with one specific correction.

Round B: paste an example of what you actually want and say "match this."

Round C: open a brand-new chat and write a first ask so good it beats both.

Compare the three results. Which move earned the most?
