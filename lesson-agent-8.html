3 · Read & play

Your question, the whole machine

Everything is built. This lesson points it at a question you choose and adds the two pieces that make it real research: the live web, and the rules for using it.

Real search, one line. The bundled mini-web taught the mechanics because it was small and checkable. For real questions, the Claude API offers search as a server-side tool: include the web-search tool in the request and the model searches the live web during its turn, returning answers with the URLs it used. Your loop doesn't change shape — search is just a tool the model requests, exactly like lesson 2, except the search itself runs on the provider's side. The notebook shows both paths: mini-web (free, keyless, deterministic) and live web (class API key).

The rules, before anything runs. First: what you send to the model — your question, your angles, anything you paste in — leaves your machine; research someone else's private situation and you have shared it. Second: the live web contains wrong pages in a way the mini-web only simulates, which is why verification is not a school exercise — it is the stage doing the most work out there. Third, the one to carry for life: the agent's report is a draft for a human, not an oracle. Before you repeat one of its claims to another person, follow the citation and read the source yourself. You built the receipts precisely so a human could spend thirty seconds using them.

Pick a question with stakes. The capstone works best on a question where you care whether the answer is right — a program you might join, a purchase your family is weighing, a local decision. Caring is what makes the verification stage feel necessary instead of ceremonial.

6 · Build

Ship the research run

Your capstone ships four things:

1. The report — half a page to a page, every factual sentence cited, plausible claims hedged, with a limits section.
2. The claim table — every claim with source, quote, and verification verdict.
3. The quarantine — refuted claims with the evidence that killed them.
4. The run report — your question, the angles used, how many pages were fetched, and one paragraph on what you would trust from this report and what you would verify by hand before repeating it.

Then the practicum move, if your question touched a real decision: give the report to the person who is deciding, and watch what they check first.
