3 · Read & play

Working memory, not a library

Ask a model "can I retake a final at Jefferson High?" and it has nothing to work with. Jefferson's handbook was never in its training data — your school's rules, your club's minutes, your own notes are invisible to it. Models that answer anyway are guessing from what other schools' handbooks tend to say, which is exactly the kind of confident wrongness you can't detect by reading the answer.

The obvious fix: paste the handbook into the chat. And for one page, that works — the model reads what you pasted and answers from it. This is the key fact of the whole course: the model answers well from text you put in front of it. What breaks is scale. The space a model can read per request — its context window — is working memory, not a library: real piles (a 200-page handbook, three years of minutes) don't fit, and even when something fits, you pay for every word sent, and burying one relevant paragraph under two hundred irrelevant pages makes answers worse, not better. Relevance gets diluted.

So the architecture that actually works keeps the pile outside the model and moves only the relevant pieces in, per question:

1. Store — your documents, split into pieces, sitting in your own storage.
2. Retrieve — for each question, find the few pieces most likely to contain the answer.
3. Ask — hand the model those pieces plus the question, with orders to answer only from them.

Steps 1 and 2 are yours — plain code, no model involved. Only step 3 touches the model at all. The next six lessons build the three steps properly; the figure shows why step 2 can't be skipped.
