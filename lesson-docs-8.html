3 · Read & play

Ship the pipeline

Everything assembles. Your pile from lesson 1 — minutes, reports, flyers, whatever drawer you claimed — goes through every stage, and you ship four things:

The table (CSV + JSON), with the quarantine file and cleaning log beside it.
Three answered questions — the ones you promised in lesson 1, each answer pointing at its rows.
The pipeline report — one page: what went in (how many documents, which formats), what each stage did (with log lines), the routing choice (which documents went to rules, which to the LLM, and why), the measured error rate, what's in quarantine and what those rows turned out to be, and the spot-check verdict.
The handoff note — three sentences a non-programmer can follow: what this table is, what it's safe to use it for, and what it must not be used for. ("Amounts marked approximate are approximate. The two quarantined months need the paper originals. Don't cite this for tax purposes.")

Privacy, before anything runs: if your pile contains real people's information — names, addresses, phone numbers — you need permission from whoever owns the documents, and anything sent to an LLM leaves your machine. When in doubt: use rules locally for the sensitive fields, or choose a public pile. A pipeline that leaks its documents has failed at the only stage that can't be rerun.

The report is the grade, because the report is the professional artifact: anyone can produce a table; the report is what lets a stranger decide how far to trust it. That's the difference between data and a rumor with columns — and it's the exact skill the practicum clients will need from you.

6 · Build

Ship it

Turn in the full capstone: table + quarantine + log, the three answered questions, the one-page pipeline report, and the three-sentence handoff note. Present five minutes: the pile, one thing a stage caught, the error rate, and one question the table answered that the drawer never could.
