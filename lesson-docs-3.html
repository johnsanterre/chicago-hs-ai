3 · Read & play

Small functions, big pile

Extraction left junk in your text: headers mid-sentence, tripled spaces, smart quotes turned into â€™ garbage, dates written four different ways. For one document you'd fix it by hand. You have forty documents this month and forty more next month — so you write cleaning functions instead, and you follow three rules:

One fix per function. strip_headers() removes page headers and nothing else. fix_spacing() collapses runs of whitespace and nothing else. Small functions are checkable — you can test each on a sample and see exactly what it does.

Compose them in order. Cleaning is a mini-pipeline inside the pipeline: each function takes text, returns text, and hands off to the next. Order matters and is worth a comment: strip headers before fixing spacing, or the header's leftover blank lines survive.

Log every change. Each function reports what it did: "removed 3 header lines," "collapsed 17 whitespace runs." The log is your alibi. When a number in the final table looks wrong, the log tells you whether cleaning touched it — and when someone asks "did processing alter the documents?", you have an answer instead of a shrug. Organizations that trust you with their documents deserve that answer.

And the habit from lesson 2 carries forward: when you find a new kind of junk, you don't fix that one document — you add one more small function, test it, and rerun the pile.
